TANTSA
Production Decision Companion for Small Food Businesses
GUESS LESS. MAKE RIGHT. EARN MORE.
One-sentence concept
Tantsa helps small prepared-food businesses decide what to prepare, how much to prepare, and when to prepare it 
using sales history, confirmed orders, available ingredients, batch constraints, and budget - then learns from actual 
outcomes to improve the next decision.
The problem  Small food businesses commit ingredients, labor, time, and cash before exact demand is known. Too 
much becomes leftover value; too little becomes a stockout and missed sale.
Core question
"Given what I know today, what should I prepare next?"
Tantsa is Tantsa is not
A daily production-planning companion A generic POS replacement
A low-data decision layer for small food businesses A guaranteed AI forecaster
Merchant-controlled and explainable A food-safety certification

Focused on what, how much, and when to prepare A consumer surplus marketplace at launch
Competition fit: Food Security / AI for Food Systems
Measurable claim: reduce avoidable prepared-food loss and improve production decisions - not "solve hunger."

1. The Problem
A repeated operating decision with financial consequences
 Overproduction - too much food is prepared before demand is known, tying cash up in unsold items.
 Stockouts - too little is prepared, products sell out early, and potential sales are missed.
 Guess-based planning - owners may rely on memory, habit, notebooks, or rough estimates.
 Fragmented information - sales, preorders, leftovers, stockouts, ingredients, batch sizes, and cash limits are rarely 
combined into one decision.
 Late reaction - slow sell-through may only be noticed near closing, when recovery options are weaker.
 Working-capital pressure - the owner may have a fixed amount of cash available for tomorrow's ingredients.
Formal problem statement
Small prepared-food businesses make production decisions under uncertain demand and resource constraints. This 
can lead to avoidable overproduction, stockouts, and inefficient use of working capital. They need an accessible 
way to turn simple business records into practical, explainable preparation decisions.
Human story
The owner commits part of tomorrow's livelihood before knowing tomorrow's demand.

Signature feature: Kasya sa Puhunan
"May P1,500 akong pambili bukas. Ano at ilan ang kaya kong ihanda?" Tantsa considers on-hand ingredients, 
current ingredient costs, menu/recipes, confirmed orders, estimated demand, batch sizes, capacity, and expected 
contribution, then proposes a feasible production mix.
 If the usual quantity of every product exceeds the budget, Tantsa can preserve confirmed orders while reducing 
lower-confidence items.
 On-hand inventory reduces tomorrow's cash purchase requirement but still has an economic cost; the app must 
keep cash requirement and product cost separate.
 For fixed-product bakeries, batch quantity and timing may matter more than budget optimization; the product 
should adapt to the segment.

What Tantsa combines
 Simple manual, voice, or photo-assisted records.
 Preorders + recent sales + budget/batch/ingredient/capacity constraints in one recommendation.
 Explicit stockout tracking so sold-out days do not teach the model the wrong demand.
 Midday reassessment instead of only next-day forecasting.
 Owner approval and understandable explanations.
 Leftover-risk action before closing, then feedback into the next cycle.

workflow fit, trusted merchant data, measurable outcomes, retention, distribution, and integrations.

9. Go-to-Market and Validation
Use real merchant behavior as the filter
Marketing message
Primary hook
"Not sure how much to prepare tomorrow?" Tantsa learns from what you made, sold, had left, and sold out - then 
helps you plan the next batch.
Channel / tactic How to use it
Direct merchant visits Ask about yesterday's leftovers and stockouts; demonstrate with 
their own numbers.
Free trial Let merchants record 1-2 weeks, then show patterns before asking 
them to pay.
Facebook / TikTok Short peso-based examples: made vs sold vs remaining, then the 
next-day decision.
Local food-business groups Reach bakeries, carinderias, stalls, cafes, and MSME communities.
Referral Merchant invites another business; use trial extension only if 
economics support it.
Partners later Negosyo Centers, coops, food associations, culinary schools, 
suppliers, POS providers.
Success stories Use measured outcomes as the strongest advertisement.
Validation plan
 Week 1: 15-20 discovery interviews across relevant segments. Collect recent leftovers, sellouts, decision method, 
budget constraints, and current records.
 Week 2: onboard up to 10 pilot merchants; measure setup time and data quality.
 Weeks 3-4: observe baseline production, leftovers, stockouts, and recording burden without recommendations.
 Weeks 5-6: turn recommendations on; track adoption, overrides, unsold ratio, stockouts, and contribution.
 After trial: offer a real paid subscription; measure conversion, renewal, and cancellation reasons.
Decision rule
If merchants do not lose enough from production mistakes to care about "what should I prepare next?", do not 
rescue the idea by adding more dashboards, AI, or marketplace features. Pivot.

11. Hard Judge Questions
Short, defensible answers
Question Defensible answer
Is it new? Not as a technology category. The innovation is the simplified low-data 
workflow that combines production planning, budget/batch constraints, 
intraday reassessment, recovery, and next-cycle learning for small 
Philippine operators.
Why not Peddlr / iCost / KwentaPOS? Those tools solve valuable POS, costing, and inventory needs. Tantsa's 
primary job is deciding what, how much, and when to prepare.
ClearCOGS / Guac already forecast prep. Yes. That validates the category. Tantsa tests a POS-light, low-data 
workflow for smaller Philippine food businesses.
What if there is no history? Start with owner baseline, confirmed orders, budget, and known 
constraints; show uncertainty until enough data exists.
What if you cause a stockout? The owner approves every plan; use ranges/buffers and explicitly track 
sellout timing and overrides.
Why the budget feature? For microbusinesses, the feasible decision may be constrained by 
tomorrow's cash, not just expected demand.
Who pays? The owner/operator. Any price survives only if measured value 
repeatedly exceeds the fee and effort.
Why Food Security? It targets avoidable prepared-food loss and production efficiency; 
measure merchant-level outcomes rather than claim it solves hunger.
Core pitch message
Tantsa helps small food-business owners turn limited cash, simple records, and uncertain demand into a practical 
daily preparation plan.

12. Risks, Boundaries, and Final Position
What can invalidate the idea
Top risks
 The pain may not be frequent or expensive enough in the first segment.
 Manual data entry may be too burdensome; voice/photo capture may still be inaccurate.
 Poor recommendations may increase stockouts.
 Competitors may add similar planning features.
 Food-safety mistakes could create serious liability.
 Savings claims may be misleading if counterfactuals are double counted.
 The test subscription price may not convert despite comparable local software pricing.
 The name Tantsa requires proper trademark clearance before commercial use.
 Multi-branch purchasing and complex integrations can distract from validating the core decision.
Measurement boundaries
 Do not count the full selling price of discarded food as money saved.
 Do not count the same unit as both prevented waste and recovered surplus.
 Weather, events, opening hours, and price changes can affect outcomes; simple before/after comparison does not 
prove causation.
 Keep actual observations separate from modeled estimates and illustrative scenarios.
Final position
Proceed with Tantsa as a production-decision companion. Keep production planning at the heart of the product; 
keep leftover recovery as a secondary layer; use Kasya sa Puhunan, batch timing, voice/photo capture, and intraday 
updates only to strengthen the core decision.
